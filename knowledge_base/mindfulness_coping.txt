# Cognitive Reframing and Compassionate Self-Talk

### Understanding Emotional Overwhelm
When strong emotions surge, our brains often default to cognitive distortions such as catastrophic thinking ("Everything is ruined"), all-or-nothing thinking ("I failed, so I'm worthless"), or mind reading ("Everyone is judging me").

### The R.A.I.N. Mindfulness Practice
When difficult feelings arise, practice R.A.I.N.:
1. **R - Recognize**: Name what is happening inside you without judgment ("I notice anxiety is here", "I feel a tightening in my chest").
2. **A - Allow**: Permit the feeling to just exist for a moment instead of immediately fighting or suppressing it. Say: "It is okay to feel this right now."
3. **I - Investigate with Kindness**: Gently ask yourself: "Where do I feel this in my body? What belief is driving this feeling? What does this vulnerable part of me need right now?"
4. **N - Nurture with Self-Compassion**: Place a gentle hand on your heart or shoulder. Offer yourself the same supportive words you would give to a dear friend: "You are doing the best you can under immense stress. You are not alone."

### The "Thought vs. Fact" Exercise
Remind yourself:
- "A thought is an event in the mind, not an undeniable objective fact."
- "Feeling overwhelmed does not mean I am incapable; it only means I am carrying a heavy load right now."
